# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

The Image Editor community welcomes contributors and users of all backgrounds
and experience levels. Everyone should be able to participate without
harassment, discrimination, or intimidation, regardless of age, disability,
ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, appearance, religion,
sexual orientation, or socioeconomic background.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with respect and empathy, including when disagreeing.
- Discuss ideas and code constructively; avoid personal attacks.
- Welcome questions, different perspectives, and contributors who are learning.
- Give and receive feedback in good faith, acknowledge mistakes, and correct harm.
- Respect people's privacy, boundaries, time, and accessibility needs.
- Help keep discussions relevant and make space for others to participate.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or encouragement of violence.
- Discriminatory language, slurs, sexual harassment, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Insults, trolling, repeated personal attacks, or deliberately disruptive conduct.
- Publishing private information or communications without permission.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or assists an investigation.
- Continuing unwanted contact after being asked to stop, including attempts to
  evade moderation decisions.

## Scope

This policy applies to project issues, pull requests, discussions, code reviews,
documentation, and other community spaces managed by the project. It also applies
when someone officially represents the project in other spaces or events.

## Reporting a concern

Report concerns privately to the project maintainer, Ben Situ, at
[sito163@163.com](mailto:sito163@163.com), with the subject **Image Editor Code of
Conduct**. Include relevant links, what happened, when it happened, and any steps
needed to protect participants. You do not need to confront the person involved
before reporting.

Do not post personal information or sensitive evidence in public issues.
Maintainers will limit access to report details to people needed to assess and
address the concern, sharing identifying information only when necessary for
safety or resolution. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

If a concern involves the maintainer, or if you prefer an independent channel for
conduct on GitHub, you can
[report abuse to GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
GitHub handles these reports under its own policies.

## Enforcement and review

Maintainers assess reports in context, consider the impact on affected people,
and may request clarification privately. Anyone involved in a report should step
aside from its review when an uninvolved maintainer is available.

Depending on severity and repetition, responses may include a request to change
behavior, a warning, removal of content, or a temporary or permanent restriction
on participation. Serious threats or harassment may result in immediate removal;
these measures do not have to occur in sequence.

A person affected by a moderation decision may request a review through the same
private email channel, including relevant new information. Reviews and responses
depend on maintainer availability; no response deadline is guaranteed.

This project policy complements the
[GitHub Community Guidelines](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-community-guidelines).
For software vulnerabilities, follow [Security Policy](SECURITY.md) instead.
